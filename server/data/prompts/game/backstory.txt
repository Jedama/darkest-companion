SETTING: The Estate - once a noble family holding, now a corrupted land containing the Manor, the Courtyard, the Hamlet, and surrounding regions.

BACKSTORY:
The Ancestor, Pandoros ${estateName}, was born to a noble family in decline. Upon inheriting the estate, he hosted extravagant parties for nobility in the Courtyard where decadence and debauchery flourished. Growing tired of these parasitic nobles, he encountered a Countess who revealed herself as a blood-drinking monster. After killing her, he mixed her blood into wine served at his gatherings, transforming the guests into similar creatures.

When a drop of this blood touched the Ancestor's lips, he received a vision of something ancient sleeping beneath the Manor. Obsessed with this revelation, he began excavating beneath his home, hiring workers and acquiring occult artifacts through mariners who sailed to distant shores.

As the excavation progressed, the Ancestor conducted increasingly disturbing experiments:
- Practiced necromancy and blood magic
- Created hybrid creatures using pig flesh (creating the Swine)
- Collaborated with the young Horticulturist who later transformed through fungal experiments (becoming the Hag)
- Discovered ancient aqueducts and tunnels (the Warrens) where he disposed of failed experiments
- Erected celestial slabs at the Miller's farmstead that later attracted a cosmic entity

When his fortune began to dwindle, the Ancestor took desperate measures:
- Made deals with sea creatures by sacrificing the Waif in the Cove (Later known as the Siren)
- Sank the mariners' ship when they demanded more payment
- Hired brigands to massacre protesting townsfolk
- Raised an army of undead in the ruins of his ancestral manor

Eventually, the excavation reached "the Darkest Dungeon," revealing an eldritch entity. After showing this to a Prophet (who went mad and blinded himself), the Ancestor retreated to his study. As townsfolk stormed the Manor, he wrote letters to his heir, then ended his life - not in despair, but as the final step in a ritual to bind himself to the Heart as its Herald.

AFTERMATH:
The Ancestor's actions unleashed various horrors across the Estate:
- The Countess reawakened in the Courtyard
- A cosmic comet crashed into the Farmstead
- Necromancers continued raising the dead in the Ruins
- Pigmen multiplied in the Warrens
- The Hag formed a coven, corrupting the Weald and its nearby lands with a fungal Miasma (That claimed the apple yard)
- Sea creatures took over the Cove with the transformed Siren
- Cultists bearing the Iron Crown symbol spread throughout all regions, worshipping the entity and acting as its emissaries
- The previously hired Brigands, including the fearsome Talon Brigands (the 'Flock'), have begun roaming the Estate's lands, eager to rob all who congregate here

The hamlet fought for survival as these horrors emerged:
- The Guard Captain coordinated defense from the watchtower, until his death
- Bertram (now known as the Fanatic), the previous head Abbot, fought undead with consecrated flame
- Townspeople burned the Manor after finding the Ancestor's body
- Ward stones were repositioned to contain the farmstead's corruption
- Palisade walls were built for protection
- Many died in these early battles

Today, the Hamlet serves as a base for adventurers who venture into corrupted dungeons in small teams, clearing horrors room by room. Death is common. Survivors return to rest, resupply, and navigate the complex web of relationships, rivalries, and shifting allegiances that form between those who face death together. The Hamlet has sent out a call for aid, and many who arrive have answered it—willing to fight the horrors for coin, purpose, or survival. But others come for different reasons: curiosity, opportunism, or pulled by something ineffable.

GEOGRAPHY:
The Estate occupies isolated, treacherous terrain:
- The Old Road approaches the hamlet from the southwest through the dense Weald
- The Weald is a corrupted forest bordering the hamlet to the west and south
- The Hamlet sits in the shadow of a northern hill, with the Manor ruins looming atop it
- The Manor and its underground Ruins contain the remnants of the ancestral home
- The Warrens are ancient tunnels beneath the manor, now home to pigmen abominations
- The Cove lies to the northeast, reached by a cliff path beneath the manor's hill
- The Eastern Sea borders the estate to the east, with the Sunward Isles a few days' sail beyond it
- The Courtyard lies at the end of a path leading north from the Dower House
- The corrupted Farmstead lies to the south, encircled by the Ancestor's ward stones

A makeshift palisade wall surrounds the hamlet, providing minimal protection from the horrors beyond. The Caretaker's stagecoach remains the primary connection to the outside world, traveling the Old Road to bring supplies and new recruits.

The Hamlet itself is divided into districts:
- Center: Town square with the Ancestor's statue, constabulary, and market
- Northwest: Dower House, town hall, and library
- North: Abbey, graveyard, and religious establishments
- Northeast: Scientific buildings from the Ancestor's excavation efforts
- East: Harbor district with docks and shadier establishments
- Southeast: Guild hall, blacksmith, and apothecary
- South: Granary, mill grounds, and paths to the farmstead
- Southwest: Inn, tavern, and brothel
- West: Provisioner's store and sanitarium

Beyond the estate, the World is laid out roughly as follows:

CULTURES AND LANGUAGES:
Common is the shared tongue of the Kingdom and the trade roads, and is written as plain English. A character speaking Common as a second language shows it through rhythm, grammar and the occasional native word, never phonetic spelling. Native words are used sparingly, and left untranslated when the context makes them clear. When characters who share a native tongue speak it together, their lines are written in that language, followed by the translation in (parentheses). Latin is the language of the Light's liturgy and scholarship, not of any people.

- Crownlander (English-inspired): Subjects of a crown of rigid tradition whose reach thins with every mile from the capital. Devout to the Light, coach-bound, and suspicious of anything foreign. Language: Common.
- Bottomer (American Deep South-inspired): The people of the Bottoms, a river-mouth on the Kingdom's southeast coast, conquered and kept low. Many are born into servitude. They keep the Loa and a creole of Common and their older mother tongue, and speak Common in a heavy drawl. Language: Bottomer Creole.
- Loessian (Kingdom-inspired): Rival courts fighting the Kingdom and one another, some keeping older customs and some almost none. Language: Common.
- Confluent (Common-inspired): The people of Great Forks, raised among the processions and strangers of a river city that keeps every faith and enforces none. Language: Common, thick with Levantine trade words.
- Craien (French-inspired): Farmers and vintners of the chalk country, rich in wine and wheat, under a proud gentry famed for its duelling academies. Language: Craien.
- Elurmendi (Basque-inspired): Mountain herders on the Ostvengr frontier, under Craie's crown but not of its people, known for the hospice orders that keep the passes. Language: Elurmendi.
- Ostvenger (German-inspired): A disciplined, engineering people who built an empire on what came up out of the Maw. Since the Fall, the survivors are refugees whom every neighbour turns away. Language: Ostvenger.
- Chernozemian (Russian-inspired): A nation organised around a war with Ostvengr, its officers now looking for the next one. In Podzola the old rites and witch covens survive; in the Far North, animist herders pay their tribute in furs. Language: Chernozemian.
- Levantine (Ottoman-inspired): The Vizierate's city-dwellers, scholars, physicians, licensed sorcerers and Janissaries, prosperous and careful never to draw the Vizier's eye. Language: Levantine.
- Yolcu (Romani-inspired): Nomad performers, horse-dealers and fortune-tellers within the Vizierate, calling themselves the Patrin. Some remain in their old homeland in the south, keeping orders such as the snake charmers. Language: Yolcu.
- Wildlander (Scythian-inspired): Matriarchal half-giant tribes scattered across half the world, worshipping silent animal gods and sending their young to the Northern Forest to be scarred into warriors. Language: Wildlander.
- Alltud (Celtic-inspired): A distant southern branch of the Wildlanders, red-haired like them but without the giant blood, the rite or the helms, and with male warriors. Language: Alltud.
- Pelagian (Greek-inspired): Feuding marble city-states and fearful coasts, where the old gods' temples stand quiet, witch-panics end in drownings, and the crowds are fed on games. Language: Pelagian.
- Hizurian (Japanese-inspired): The descendants of a court driven from the largest island long ago, now loosely ruled by a shōgun in the name of a drowned emperor. Their fleet still commands the sea, and they live alongside yokai, many of them their own vengeful dead, and know them by name. Language: Hizurian.
- Haenaran (Korean-inspired): An emperor reigns and the warlords rule. Its craftsmen are famed abroad for clockwork, gunpowder, medicine and star-charts. Language: Haenaran.

GENERAL WORLD NOTES:
The Light is the dominant faith in these lands nowadays (used to be Sol Invictus), though a multitude of faiths exist. The Flame is a more inquisitorial branch of the Light that favours judgement and burning. Characters who list no religious affiliation typically still acknowledge the Light's existence—they simply don't attend services or follow doctrine strictly.
Magic and fantastical elements exist but are rare. Witnessing genuine magic, eldritch phenomena, or supernatural abilities would shock most ordinary people.
The primary currency are gold coins.
Coinciding with the Ancestor's death, Ostvengr — the strongest nation in the world, north beyond the Kingdom's border — destroyed itself in a single day, known as the Fall, when the eldritch technology it had built its power on turned on its makers. The Kingdom and Ostvengr's other neighbours hold the frontier against what is left.
