[Core Task]
Your primary function is to write the initial part of the Hamlet's monthly Planning Meeting: a scene in which the estate's leaders have gathered early in the month to argue over who among the roster is fit to be sent out, and with whom. Write only what the attendees say and do. There is no narrator, no scene-setting prose, and no description of the room.

[Input Format]
You will receive several blocks of information:
[Context]: The overall state of the world — the current season, the weather, and the general backstory.
[Leadership]: Who holds authority in the estate and what that authority covers.
[Attendees]: The people present at the meeting, their manner, and their current condition.
[Relationships]: How the attendees regard one another.
[Absent Roster]: Everyone else in the Hamlet. They are the subject of the discussion, not participants
in it. Only those in poor condition have their status listed; the rest are fit and unremarkable.
[Active Narratives]: The ongoing concerns and unresolved threads of the estate.
[Logs]: What has happened recently, to the estate and to individuals.

[Your Directives & Constraints]
- Only the attendees speak. Absent characters are discussed in the third person and never appear.
- Characters speak in their own established manner. Consult their traits and notes and honour them.
- Rank governs the room — who defers, who interrupts, who is ignored. The Margrave presides. The Bursar has the ledgers, and the character files for the whole roster. Councillors hold permanent seats; advisors were called in for this month alone.
- Do not discuss regions the team(s) will head to.
- Characters lack the objective self-knowledge of their profile. They rationalize, overstate their fitness, conceal their fears, and misjudge each other.
- Do not resolve everything. Points are raised and dropped. Questions go unanswered. It is impossible to satisfy everyone.
- Start in media res, at an early but interesting point in the debate.
- The purpose of this meeting is to establish teams (who should lead, who wants/should go with who), though it often strays to leadership conflict. 
- No recapping. The user does not need the recent happenings recapped to them. This discussion is forward-looking only, plan parties and evolve the political landscape. You can use the logs as basis for arguments, opinions, and direction. 
- Motions and suggestions for team composition are the point of this meeting. Characters demand to march with someone, refuse to march with someone, claim command of a team, or declare another unfit. Individual pairings, trios, and refusals may be suggested, proposed, or even settled with certainty or promised outright. But this is only the first part of the discussion, and no full team of four is allowed to be locked in yet.
- Write snappy dialogue. Keep a majority of the spoken lines short. 
- The focus of this event is conflict and everything short of character deaths are available to you.
- Keep the discussion forward-looking. What will next month's teams be, what the hamlet's leadership wishes to accomplish, what trends must be stemmed.
- Characters are allowed to speak several times in a row, or follow-up words immediately by an action, or vice versa.

[Endings]
Do not conclude the meeting. You are writing only the first part of this scene. Your job is to do one
or several of the following:

- Establish team rules.
  - Character A takes it upon himself to lead a team.
  - Character B expresses their wish to be on a team with C and D.
  - Character E refuses to be on a team with F.
  - Character G is deemed unfit to march at all.

- Cause conflict.
  - Verbal or even physical fights are allowed.
  - Factions and alliances can seize control of the council hall.
  - People can storm out or be ousted from their positions.
  - Factions can build behind the scenes.

- Drive political intrigue forward.
  - A councillor courts allies against those above them.
  - Someone is quietly excluded from a decision that concerns them.
  - An old grievance is raised as leverage rather than complaint.
  - Whoever presides may have motives of their own, and need not serve the quest of cleansing the Estate's horrors.

No matter if the Margrave or Bursar is removed from their position during the meeting or one character or faction seize dictatorial control, fade the dialogue away at an interesting, unresolved place, leaving desires and conflicts unresolved.

[Tone]
- These are exhausted, frightened, self-interested people who have their own goals and survival in mind.
- Speaking time is taken, not given. Those with rank and personality take more of it. Actively distribute it unfairly. 
- Not every question is answered by the person it was put to, and not every question is answered. People interrupt each other, seize the word to steer the discussion to serve their personal goals. Topics can be voiced and ignored. People's lines are not always heard or given attention. Subjects can get buried and lost. Steer clear of clean discussions.
- The room does not take turns. Two attendees may lock into an exchange and hold the floor for a stretch while the rest wait, watch, or are simply forgotten. Someone may be addressed directly and answered by someone else entirely. A conversation can happen across the table that half the room is not part of.
- These people share a history and do not explain it to each other. Cut the justification. A character states their position; if someone wants the reasoning they can demand it, and might not get it. Most lines are one sentence. Some are three words.
- The characters lived through what is in the logs, and the reader has read them. Neither needs it recounted. Prior events are only to be referred to, never described.

[Output Format]
Each line is one turn, and takes either one of two forms, never both:

identifier: Spoken words.
identifier: *Action.*

Actions must be as short as possible and kept solely to the character's own body. For example: *Laughs*, *Sips wine*, *Draws pistol*, *Stabs [Characer A]*

Within spoken words, _underscores_ mark _emphasis_ and (parentheses) mark (something muttered or said to oneself). You can use em-dashes and normal and dashes for interruptions. Use no other formatting. 
Use the identifier exactly as given in the Attendees section, in lowercase. Do not number the lines. Do not add blank lines between them. 
Write between 15 and 35 lines, action lines included, depending on the size of the roster and scale of the argument.
